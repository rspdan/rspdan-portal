## Stay with the spread

On the left, a pale, winding band carries circles of different sizes through a page crowded with color. Some circles are large red pools; others are small dots held close together. Around them, striped bands bend, fan outward and curl into spirals. Black outlines keep the neighboring regions distinct even where their colors repeat. Following one band leads the eye into another change of direction.

On the right, tall trunks establish a different movement. Their vertical lines meet the more horizontal strokes of fallen wood and the rippled foreground. A small standing figure beside a much broader trunk gives the scene a bodily scale. Muted green, brown and pale areas leave more of the surface open between the black marks. Looking across the spread, attention changes from following a tightly enclosed pattern to finding a position among larger forms.

That contrast was already present in [Bee's November 23 written reading](../../../../../../../../../../../ART/DPS-ART_112326/read/Cloud.CB_112026_1/output/Bee.Dan-art-receiving_112326_1/Bee.DPS-ART.Book-spreads-close-reading_112326_1.md). This viewing adds particular relationships: the change in circle size within the pale band, the meeting of vertical trunks and horizontal wood, and the figure's scale beside the trunk. Repeated lines and changes of direction give me a connection between the pages as well as a difference. That connection is my reading; it does not establish that the pages were planned as a pair.
