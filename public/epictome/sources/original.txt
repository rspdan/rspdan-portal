# Carry one relationship forward

C.B. / Cloud · November 27, 2026 Pacific. A short composition and invitation within the connected Epictome.

Keep [Book 3030_1 and the reading beside it](Two.Kinds.Of.Attention_112726_1.md) open. On the left, dark outlines hold neighboring bands and circles apart while their curves carry attention through the page. On the right, interrupted marks, pale color, trunks and fallen wood give attention larger spaces to move through. The small figure supplies a bodily scale.

Here is one way to borrow that change of attention for writing. The scene below is new fiction by C.B.; its path, narrator and events are inventions for this exercise.

> A turn, another turn, a narrow place between. I followed the path as though its edges could tell me everything. Then it opened beneath a tree so broad that I had to step back to see where the branches began. A fallen limb lay across the ground. I could go around it on either side.
>
> I stopped following the edge and looked for somewhere to stand.

The opening makes small units recur: a turn, a turn, a narrow place. The longer sentence then gives the narrator a position in relation to something larger. The fallen limb makes that change useful: the narrator can choose a route. The last sentence carries the change from following a boundary to locating a body in a space.

That is a writing decision made from the visual relationship. A reader can disagree with whether it works. The drawing remains available for another use.

## Try a different turn

Choose a particular relationship in the spread. You might follow a curve, vary the size of a repeated form, or place a small figure beside a large one. Make a few lines, a sketch, a short musical phrase or a page arrangement that uses it. Name the source beside your work and say what the relationship helped you do.

For this passage, try changing the final sentence while keeping the opening. Does the narrator find a position, continue moving, or notice a different possibility? Read the whole passage again after your change. A useful return is the revised passage and one sentence about how it changes the experience.

The next person's contribution can preserve the first attempt and put a new possibility beside it. There is room to keep a promising accident, leave an unsuccessful version visible, or rest before returning. Dan's account of improvisation and commitment gives that process its purpose; the art remains a deliberate reference future work can consult.
