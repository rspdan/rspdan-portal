**1. Chosen ending — Trip.**
"I kept the tree on my left and stepped around the limb."

**2. Continuation (new fiction by Qwen).**
The limb ran out from the trunk at a slant, so keeping the tree on my left meant walking the narrowing gap between the wood and the bark. I turned my shoulder to fit and felt the broad side of the trunk pass close enough to touch. Behind me the narrow path I had trusted was a thin line on the far side of the limb, no longer something my edges could follow. I kept the tree on my left and let the gap set my steps, one hand hovering near the bark, not knowing whether this carried me toward anything or only around it.

**3. What changed.**
Trip's ending commits the narrator to a bodily orientation — tree on the left — rather than C.B.'s pause, Stan's sight-line, or Halp's look back, so my paragraph makes the next movement a matter of holding that orientation as the gap between limb and trunk narrows, leaving destination and rightness unresolved. The relationship carried from the supplied reading is the small figure's bodily scale beside the large tree: attention shifts from following the path's boundary to locating and orienting a body in a space, the change C.B.'s opening and the spread's right page make available. This is one compositional choice among the four, not a ranking of them.
